1. PALETTE (from the manual; contrast ratios measured by me)
- Ink #1A1A1A: type, sport images, dark fields. 17.4:1 on white.
- Graphite #3A3A37: secondary text. 11.4:1 on white.
- White #FFFFFF, Mist #F7F7F5, Sand #F0EFEC: page grounds, cards, section bands.
- ASNM Orange #C5430C: primary buttons, links, selected state, focus, active map pin. 5.0:1 on white (4.7 on Mist). White text on this orange passes AA even at body size. The manual says to verify that; I did.
- Orange Ink #A8370A: small orange text. 6.5:1.
- Orange Soft #FBEBDC: callouts, selected chips.
- Logo Flame #E85D04: the logo and big decorative accents only. 3.5:1, so never use it for text.
- Signal Teal #0E6F73: optional data or status color, never in the logo. 5.9:1.
- Muted text (live site) #6E6D6A: 5.2:1 on white, 4.8:1 on Mist.
- Balance: 70-80% neutrals, 15-25% Ink and Graphite, 5-10% orange, 0-5% teal. In the manual's words: "Orange is a signal, not wallpaper." No navy-and-gold pairing.

2. TYPE
- DM Sans is the only family, in weights 400, 500, 600 and 700. The server pages already load it from Google Fonts. Fallbacks: Arial, Helvetica, system-ui, sans-serif. No condensed, script, novelty or distressed fonts.
- Sizes:
  - Display: 700, 40-64px, tight leading.
  - H1: 700, 28-40px.
  - H2: 600-700, 20-28px.
  - Body: 400-500, 16-18px, line height 1.45-1.65. Use 17-18px to match Alec's taste.
  - Label: 600-700, 12-14px. Uppercase only for short labels.
  - Data: 500-700 with tabular numerals.
- Headings and buttons in sentence case. Never more than 5 words in all caps. Body lines about 45-75 characters. Inputs at least 16px so iPhones don't zoom.

3. LOGO
- The official mark is the black-and-orange athlete: an A with a wheelchair athlete, legs in orange. File: asnm-launch/public/favicon.svg (#1A1A1A plus #E85D04). It is the same athlete as the 2026 Adapt To Life logo (emblems/atl-logo-2026.svg, black only) and the one-color stamp emblems/adaptive.svg.
- Allowed forms: full color on white, Mist or a quiet light photo. Reversed (white) and black one-color versions only from approved master files. A small icon version only if an approved small master exists.
- Clear space: 25% of the logo's width on every side. Minimum height 32px on screen, 48px preferred.
- Placement: top-left in the interface, centered as a profile or cover image, bottom-right as a quiet sign-off on artwork.
- NEVER: redraw, recolor (gradient, metallic, neon, fire, camo), crop, rotate, stretch, outline, add shadow or animate it. Never put it inside or merge it with a map pin, a letter or a sport object. Never put the letters ASNM inside it. Never place it over faces, equipment or busy textures.
- Naming: "Adaptive Sports Near Me" on first use, ASNM after that. Logo alt text: "Adaptive Sports Near Me".
- Artistic versions (ember, adventure, court, stadium, hero) are artwork, not logos. They never go in the site header, favicon, profile image or sponsor lockup. When one is the hero image, a quiet official logo or name must appear too.
- Where the live site differs: the header is text only ("Adaptive Sports Near Me", DM Sans 700 at 16px) with no mark, while the manual puts the mark top-left. The manual has no approved wordmark lockup. The only lockup I found is in the off-system Brand Kit.

4. VOICE
- Useful, human, clear, respectful, optimistic without pretending, inviting, credible. Sound like a knowledgeable person, not an institution. Manual line: "Useful first, hopeful always, never patronizing."
- Promise: "Find your place in adaptive sports." (voice.md, the newer source). Manual's main message: "Your place in adaptive sports may be closer than you think." Tagline: "Find. Explore. Connect."
- Shape: hook, value, proof, then one action. One main call to action per screen.
- Call-to-action families: Find, View, Attend, Apply, Contribute, Correct, Subscribe, Share. The manual's button examples: Find a program, Explore sports, List your organization, Share an event, Find equipment and funding, Tell us what is missing.
- Tone by place: search and listings are short and neutral. Welcome and social are warm. Safety is calm and specific. Errors are respectful, say what to do, and never blame the user.
- Numbers: numerals for dates, distances, ages and costs. Dates in prose read like "July 22, 2026".
- Words, prefer vs avoid:
  - "wheelchair user", not "wheelchair-bound"
  - "person with a disability" or "disabled person" (as they prefer), not "the disabled"
  - "accessible" or "adaptive", not "special needs"
  - "participates in", not "suffers from"
  - "non-disabled", not "normal" or "able-bodied"
  - "athlete", "participant" or "newcomer", not "inspiration", "hero" or "warrior"
- DO and DON'T examples:
  - Headline: DO "Find adaptive sports near you". DON'T "Unleash the unstoppable warrior within".
  - Button: DO "Find a program" or "Visit website". DON'T "Click here".
  - Label: DO "Sport or activity". DON'T "What are you interested in maybe trying?"
  - Error: DO "Enter a city, state, or ZIP code". DON'T "Invalid input".
  - Empty result: DO "Nothing in Tampa yet. Here are programs elsewhere in Florida." DON'T "No results".
  - Claims: DO "This directory is new and still growing". DON'T "the nation's most complete directory".
- DON'T: pity, inspiration for its own sake, bragging, corporate talk, false urgency, implying Adapt To Life runs the programs, implying the list covers the whole country, sounding like a tech pitch, counting or bragging about the mission, em dashes, codes or jargon. No money ask anywhere.

5. IMAGERY AND SPORT IMAGES
- The manual's own sport images are 11 3D equipment drawings (three-quarter view, soft shadow, orange accents), and its social posts lead with photos. Alec's flat set replaces both as the sport images.
- Manual rules that still apply to Alec's set:
  - One style per grid. Never mix 3D equipment, photos, emoji and flat icons in one grid, so the old 3D icons and the 11 photos come out of any grid that uses the new set.
  - Keep scale and visual center the same across the set. The manual frames the object at 72-84% of the square. Alec's 6% margin gives about 88%; just keep it the same everywhere.
  - Transparent master files, shown on white or Mist cards.
  - Every image needs a sport label and alt text that names the sport. Use empty alt when a text label right next to it says the same thing.
  - Check the whole set as a contact sheet at 24, 80 and 240px before approval.
  - Show the sport, not a generic disability symbol. Don't use a disability symbol to stand for every audience.
  - No AI-made athletes shown as real people. No orange tint over photos. Image corners 12-20px.
  - Photos (if any): real people taking part, with agency, a mix of ages, races, genders, disabilities and regions. Youth in about 10-20% of athlete images, with a guardian release. No pity, medical framing or "overcoming" clichés.
- What I saw in the new set (contact sheet: /tmp/asnm-brand/sheet.png):
  - It is drawn in two styles. The bold brushstroke athlete that matches the logo reads cleanly at 32px: basketball, tennis, archery, golf, pickleball, curling, fencing, boccia, baseball, shooting, martial arts, dance, climbing. The detailed drawings, with spoked wheels or several people, blur at 32px: multi, soccer, football, softball, lacrosse, handball, event, grant, veteran meetup, art therapy, powerlifting (see /tmp/asnm-brand/tiny.png). Rule: at least 64px on cards. At chip or map-marker size (40px or less), use a simpler symbol or the sport name.
  - Multi-sport is 38% of listings (529) and is a busy three-person drawing. Surfing reuses it, and five more sports share pictures: blind sports uses goalball's; equestrian vaulting and equine therapy use horseback riding's; handcycling uses cycling's; wheelchair racing uses track and field's. Result grids will repeat images, so type and place have to carry the variety.
  - Most figures use wheelchairs. Amputee sports, running, goalball, blind sports, swimming and Nordic skiing don't. Don't use the wheelchair figure as the generic "everyone" icon in the nav or hero.
  - The set is black only, while the manual's icons had orange accents. Use the files as delivered and keep orange in the interface.

6. WEBSITE AND UI (from the manual)
- Corner radii 8, 12, 16, 20px. Pill shapes only where they mean something. Subtle borders and shadows, never decorative depth. Content up to 1280px wide, gutters 24-48px.
- Core journey:
  1. Search by place and sport.
  2. Compare listings with the same details on each.
  3. Open a listing: dates, cost, ages, equipment, access, source.
  4. Go to the provider.
  5. Come back to results with filters and map position kept.
- Program card: sport, provider, location, cost, status, audience, equipment, date updated.
- Filter chips: text label plus a selected mark, removable without reopening the panel.
- Map: clusters and the selected marker differ by shape and label, not color alone. The pin is a working symbol, orange when active, and never merged with the logo.
- Drawers and pop-ups: a named heading, focus kept inside, Escape and a close button, focus returned on close.
- Phone: search, result count, filters and the list/map switch all reachable with one hand.
- Trust: last-checked date, source, verification status in plain words, a way to report a correction.
- Status labels:
  - Open: Ink or teal, with a check.
  - Seasonal: orange, with a calendar.
  - Waitlist: Orange Ink, with a clock.
  - Unavailable: muted.
  - Error: red, with an icon.
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA. Contrast 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for large text and controls. Tap targets 44x44. Works at 200% zoom. Honor reduced-motion settings, no flashing, and motion never carries meaning; this applies to prototype D's pulsing dot. Never use color alone for status.

7. SOCIAL (Instagram)
- Feed posts 4:5, stories 9:16. One idea per post. Headline 3-8 words, at most 20 words on the image. One small orange accent, the official logo in the same corner every time, one action.
- The template slot is "one strong photo or approved equipment icon". Once Alec approves his set, his sport image fills that slot.
- Series: Sport Spotlight (sport image, "Explore [sport]") and "50 States. One Community." (state plus a map cue, "Explore programs in [state]").
- Lock the logo, margins, type and colors. Only the image, headline, location and action change from post to post.

**Recommendation**
1. **Feed cards: two-tone (T8).** The athlete is drawn in the family's deep color on that family's soft tint, in a 4:3 plate with 16px radius and optical sizing on. It looked the most premium and cohesive, stays white-paper, and reads at 2 columns on a phone. The quieter fallback is T1: ink #1A1A1A on the soft tint.
2. **Dense "near you" results on phone: list row (T5).** Text on the left, an 84px square tinted plate on the right.
3. **Wow surfaces only (listing hero, featured, Instagram share card): inverted (T3) and big crop (T6).** Inverted is a white athlete on the deep color. Big crop draws the athlete larger and bleeds it off the bottom of a deep plate, with the sport named top-left. Use big crop only when the plate is at least 280px wide; at 2-column phone width the label crowds the athlete.
4. **Skip:**
   - T2 (white plate with a color rule): plain.
   - T7 (ink plate): reads as dark mode, against Alec's white-paper taste.
   - White athlete on brand orange #FF5C39: only 3.1:1.
   - T4 (state badge): works, but the place is already in the text. Keep it for state pages only.

**Optical sizing**
Every file is fitted by its longest side, so wide pictures draw far less ink:
- Bounding box area: goalball/blindsports 30%, cycling 37%, swimming 39%, football 79%.
- Ink coverage: 9.3% to 34.1%.

The fix is a size-container plate plus per-key numbers (all 47 are measured in the lab's OPT table):
- `--w`, `--h`: bbox width and height as fractions of the canvas.
- `--g`: sqrt(w×h).
- `--dk`: a density trim, (0.31/density)^0.3 clamped to 0.90 to 1.04, so dense group scenes draw slightly smaller and thin line art slightly bigger.

CSS that worked:
```css
.plate{container-type:size;aspect-ratio:4/3;display:grid;place-items:center}
.fig{aspect-ratio:1;width:62%;width:min(calc(var(--A,.6)*100cqh/var(--g)*var(--dk,1)),calc(var(--Hm,.8)*100cqh/var(--h)),calc(var(--Wm,.84)*100cqw/var(--w)))}
```
- List row: A .66, Hm .84, Wm .88.
- Hero (16:10 plate): A .62, Hm .78.

This made goalball and handcycling roughly twice as present while football came down a little. A shared floor line (`translate:0 calc(38cqh - var(--h)*50%)`) made almost no difference, so use centered. The table should live in listing-art.js (for example `ListingArt.OPTICAL[key]`) and both renderers should write it as an inline style.

**Size scale (judged at 2x DPR)**
- Hero: 240 to 320px.
- Card: 4:3 plate, 160 to 300px wide.
- List row badge: 84px.
- Chip for group scenes: 56px minimum.
- Sport rail or filter: 48px. The rail includes multi, which is 38% of listings and blurs at 40px.
- 24 to 32px: bold single athletes only, always next to a text label.
- Share card: 1080×1350.

Bold logo-style athletes read at 24 to 32px. The detailed scenes (event, veteran-meetup, art-therapy, grant, multi, soccer, football, lacrosse, handball, softball, volleyball, triathlon, nordicskiing) blur below 48px. Trimming the built-in 6% margin (scale 1.12) helps only a little.

**Recoloring**
Use a CSS mask, painted from `currentColor`:
```css
.fig{background:currentColor;-webkit-mask:var(--src) center/contain no-repeat;mask:var(--src) center/contain no-repeat}
```
with `<i class="fig" style="--src:url(/emblems/sports/KEY.svg)" aria-hidden="true">`.
- One cached file per sport serves any color. All 47 SVGs total 278 KB raw (tennis is 1.5 KB gzipped, event 8 KB).
- Inline SVG also works: CSS `g{fill:currentColor}` overrides the `fill="#1A1A1A"` attribute. It's heavier, so use it for a single hero.
- A plain `<img>` is black only. Pure white needs `filter:brightness(0) invert(1)`; `invert(1)` alone gives #E5E5E5.
- Keep the PNGs for email and og:image.

**Accessibility**
- The image is decorative wherever text names the sport (cards, rail, hero): `alt=""` or `aria-hidden`. Never repeat descriptive alt text on every card; with 1,396 listings that is noise for screen readers.
- An icon-only control gets `aria-label` set to the sport name. Share images get og:image:alt such as "Wheelchair tennis, Adaptive Sports Near Me".
- Color is never the only signal, because the sport is always named in text.
- Muted text on tints must be #5f5d59 or darker. The current #6c6a66 is 4.43:1 on the house tint, which fails AA.
- Under forced-colors, add `forced-color-adjust:none; background:CanvasText` to the mask figure.

**Pitfalls**
1. `ListingArt.symbolSrc('grant')` and `symbolSrc('event')` return multi.svg, because `HAS_ART` only holds the 45 sport keys. My first render put the multi-sport picture on a grant card. Use `GRANT_ART`/`EVENT_ART`, or add grant and event to `HAS_ART`.
2. `FAMILY` has no `multi` entry. It falls back to house, which is fine, but it means 38% of cards are house-toned.
3. Several sports share one picture:
   - equestrian, equestrianvaulting and equine-therapy
   - goalball and blindsports
   - cycling and handcycling
   - trackfield and wheelchairracing
   - multi and surfing

   Side by side in a browse-by-sport grid they look like a bug.
4. Bottom-bleed crops cut ground-level props: soccer ball, sled hockey puck, golf tee, boccia balls, curling stone, goalball ball. The lab's `FLOOR_PROP` set gives those seven keys no bleed, and the props survived.
5. Some images carry their ink to the left of center: fencing (-0.156), shooting (-0.12), soccer and boccia (-0.10). Centering on the bounding box still looks fine; an optional fix is translateX up to +5%.
6. Container query units need Safari 16 or later. The plain `width:62%` declaration is the fallback.
7. Raw `sportLabel` can be very long ("Sports, games, recreation, camps and Special Olympics training"). Card sub-lines need a short sport name derived from the key.
8. At 2 columns on a phone, the full "Verified · checked Oct 8, 2026" line wraps to 2 or 3 lines. Shorten it on cards.

ASNM PATTERN BRIEF: Airbnb, Reddit, and three adaptive sports finders on a phone (researched 2026-10-14)

THE SIX CALLS THAT MATTER
1. Airbnb's icon row is right for us, but the 24px icon size is wrong. I rendered Alec's set at 24/32/40/48/64/96px (/tmp/asnm-pattern/sizes.png). At 24px, basketball, tennis, baseball, golf and archery all look like the same wheelchair figure, because the ball, racquet or bat shrinks to a speck. The sport starts to read at about 40px. Multi, soccer, event and grant need 48px. Prototype D's rail used 30px, which is too small. Use a 44px icon on phones and 48px on desktop, always with a label underneath.
2. Airbnb dropped its category bar in April 2025. The categories were assigned by machine learning and were often wrong; Airbnb replaced them with Homes/Experiences/Services tabs and animated 3D icons. The lesson for us: an icon row only works when the category is a hard fact. Sport is a hard fact. Our version of a "wrong category" is the multi-sport catch-all (529 listings, 38%). In the prototype snapshot, 57 multi listings name exactly one sport in their "Sports and activities:" text. For example, ACEing Autism is really tennis. Retag those, and for true multi-sport listings show up to 3 small sport icons instead of the generic multi picture.
3. The picture tells you the sport. When the page already says the sport, shrink the picture. Airbnb cards work because every photo is different. Ours repeat by sport: a "tennis near Tampa" list of big cards would show the same tennis picture over and over. Reddit solves the same problem inside a community: posts in r/tennis don't repeat the community avatar (my observation of current Reddit, not a cited source). So: lists that mix sports (home "Near you", state pages, events) get Airbnb cards with the sport picture. Lists of one sport (sport pages, a filtered search) get compact Reddit-style rows: a 64px tile, or no picture at all.
4. Lead with results, not a form. Every adaptive finder I checked makes people fill in forms or pick a state before showing anything. Airbnb and Reddit show content right away. Land on results for a place, and make the sport rail filter instantly, with no Apply button.
5. A date on every listing is our edge, because none of the three finders show one. But in the prototype snapshot, 123 of the 921 verified listings have no lastChecked date, and 236 of all 1,396 have none. That data has to be fixed, or those cards need fallback wording ("Listed <date>"), before opening day. This is one of the opening-day bars.
6. Use black, not Airbnb pink. The main action ("Visit website") is a black button on white. The sport family tones (court, racquet, field, snow, water, trail, calm) bring the color.

Category icon bar (Airbnb 2022-2025)

  - Always show the label. Many of our icons share the same wheelchair-athlete outline, so the label is what tells them apart.

- AVOID: 3D versions of the athletes. The flat black mark is the brand.

Search pill
- Airbnb: a pill (9999px radius, 64px tall on desktop, 48px search button) floating at the top. Tapping it opens a full-screen search with skippable steps (Where / When / Who) and example answers like "Any week".
- ADAPT:
  - One pill, about 56px tall, full width minus 16px margins, hairline border plus a soft shadow, reading "Sport · Place".

Listing card

    - Name: 18px/700, 2 lines max.

- Rows for single-sport lists:
  - 64px tile (or no picture), then the same text lines.
  - About 5 rows fit on a phone screen, against about 1.5 Airbnb-style cards.

Listing page

- ADAPT:

    - Description.
    - "Suggest an update" link.

  - Sticky bottom bar: 72px plus the phone's safe-area inset.

    - "Suggest an update" stays out of the bar.

Map toggle
- Airbnb app: a full map behind a list that drags up and down (peek / half / full). The map and list stay in sync. Mobile web: a floating "Map" pill.
- ADAPT:
  - Floating pill at the bottom center: 48px tall, black, white 15px/700 "Map" with a map icon, 24px above the safe area. It flips to "List".

3) ADAPTIVE SPORTS FINDERS: what they get wrong on phones

Move United, moveunitedsport.org/locations/ (fetched 2026-10-14)

USOPC Paralympic Sport Club finder
- A "Choose a state" dropdown leads to a state page. Each club is a name, a paragraph and a website link.

USTA "Wheelchair programs across the country"
- One long text page grouped by USTA section (Caribbean, Eastern, Florida...), which is the organization's internal geography.

- Results come before forms.
- Sport icons on every card.
- Plain words, never organization labels.
- A date on every listing.
- Distance is honest and in order: in your city, then about 25 mi, then elsewhere in your state, then statewide.
- No personal contacts (matches Faisal's privacy change). "Visit website" is the action.
- Light pages: 20 results per page, server-rendered, and the 3-18 KB sport SVGs lazy-loaded below the first screen.
- Never an empty page: fall back to statewide, then show "Help us build it".
