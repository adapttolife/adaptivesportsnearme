Finding an adaptive sports program usually starts with a dozen browser tabs. A club two towns over, a hospital rec-therapy page that has not been updated in years, a Facebook group that may or may not be active. If you have done that search, you know the feeling.

We built [Adaptive Sports Near Me](https://adaptivesportsnearme.com) to shorten it. It is a free directory of adaptive sports programs across the United States. Pick a sport, wheelchair basketball, sled hockey, goalball, adaptive skiing, wheelchair tennis and more, then look by city or state. Each listing shows who runs the program, where it meets, and how to reach them.

## Start with the sport, not the paperwork

Most programs welcome first-timers and many run loaner equipment nights, so you can try a sport before owning anything. If a listing looks close but not quite right, reach out anyway. Coaches in adaptive sport tend to know every other program within a hundred miles, and they hand people to each other all the time.

## The gear question

Adaptive equipment is expensive. A basketball chair can run several thousand dollars, and a sit-ski more. Several national funds exist to cover exactly this, and most accept applications on a rolling basis:

- **Challenged Athletes Foundation**, up to $5,000, rolling
- **Kelly Brush Active Fund**, up to $5,000, March 1 and September 1
- **Move United Equipment Fund**, varies, rolling
- **High Fives Empowerment Grant**, up to $7,500, rolling
- **Travis Roy Quality of Life Grant**, up to $5,000, spring and fall

A strong application usually names the program you plan to join. That is one more reason to find your local club first, and one more thing the directory is for.

New programs as they join the directory, application windows for funds like the ones above, and occasionally a closer look at one sport and how to get started in it. If someone forwarded this to you, you can subscribe at [adaptivesportsnearme.com](https://adaptivesportsnearme.com).
